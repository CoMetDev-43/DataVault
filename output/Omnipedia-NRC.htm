NRC

NRC or Nile River Coalition refers to the diplomatic grouping of
the countries Sudan, South Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya and Uganda.
Originally formed around a set of trading treaties between South
Sudan and Ethiopia in 2022, Sudan would sue to join in 2024.

As Global Warming trends continued through the late 2020's, the

NRC's principle focus shifted to water trading tariffs as the
neighboring countries sought to assist each other with their
increasing water shortages.

In June of 2030 with the signing of the Kampala agreement,
Uganda joined, swiftly followed by Kenya in September.

Throughout the 2030s, the NRC began to draw up plans for dams
and water treatment plants along the Nile River, as well as along
its tributaries that passed through their territories. These plans
drew discord from Egypt which voiced concerns that the plans
were in violation of international law, particularly the 1996
Helsinki Rules on the Uses of the Waters of International Rivers,
however as the NRC had no means to fund the construction of
projects on that scale, no motions of formal objection were passed.

In 2039, in the wake of substantial new finds of the Rare Earth
Elements niobium and neodymium, Kenya signed extensive
exclusive trade deals with the CDP, in exchange for membership
of that Mutual Defense Pact.

Kenya's membership of the CDP would lead to the remainder of
the NRC joining in 2040, paving the way for the green-lighting of
their plans for their dam and water purification facilities.

The projects would not only be funded by the CDP, but large
influxes of expertise and staff from other CDP signatory nations
would be brought to bear on them too.

Despite Egyptian protestations, as well as harsh words from the
central committees of the Winslow Accord, the construction
projects would continue. Kenya brought its facilities fully online
in 2053, with Sudan and South Sudan following suit in 2055.

Egypt would claim a noticeable drop in the levels of the Nile as a
direct result. It is generally thought that only the promise of the

Winslow Accord funded Sub-Cairo-Aquifer Development Project
started in 2048 that averted the threat of war in the region at this
time.

In 2059 Ethiopia completed their construction efforts.

Facing severe drought and under increasing threat of an armed
uprising at home, Egypt's military rulers declared war on the NRC,
their stated goal; the destruction of all of the NRC dams.

This would turn out to be a significant miscalculation on the part
of the Egyptians, as the military coalition with which they locked
horns turned out to be a substantially modernized, well equipped
and well trained fighting force, as a result of the NRC's
membership of the CDP.

The NRC /Egyptian conflict would continue in a state of deadlock
for the next 4 years, until following the failure of the Aquifer
Project in 2062, a parched and tired Egyptian army would retreat
back into their own territories in 2063 swiftly followed by an NRC
army which very much had momentum on its side.

What followed was the siege of Cairo which continues to this day;
the NRC controls much of Egypt's territory, and most of Cairo
itself has fallen or appears to be on the verge of collapse.

In 2064, Uganda completed work on all of its water processing
facilities. Winslow Accord monitors working in the area report
that the flow of the Nile through the Egyptian capital city has been
reduced to 10% of its volume of only 50 years prior.
