Masud Assaf

Refugees 27.05.63

The refugees keep pouring into our camp.

They are women and children for the most part, some of them wounded or
beaten by the cowardly invaders. I am not sure we have much more room
left in camp for any more. Our unit has voluntarily given up our bunks for now
but our commander keeps demanding that we take them back to rest at
night. The men can't seem to manage the strength to tell these poor
unfortunates to get out of their bunks.

We are tired, but haven't seen the difficulties of these people.

I saw a woman who looked just like Aunt Aziza. She was crying and holding
her daughter. It reminded me how important it is to keep fighting.

When I spoke with her all she could do is cry for her husband. She told me
that the men of their towns keep fighting but the enemy is relentless and
brutal.

Most of them were fighting with old guns that were no match for the NRC
weapons or vehicles.

I told her that I would do everything in my power to stop these invaders.

I am not sure it comforted her much.
