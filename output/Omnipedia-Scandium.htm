Scandium

Name: Scandium

Symbol: Sc

Atomic Number: 21

Category: transition metal

Discovered in 1879 by spectral analysis techniques in samples of
minerals obtained in Scandinavia, low availability and difficulties
in the process of production meant that samples of metallic scandium
were not obtained until 1937.

Scandium is classified as a rare earth element although is not a
member of the lanthanide group. In its metallic form it presents
as a soft silver/white metal, which forms yellow or pinkish oxides
when exposed to air.

Russia produces the bulk of the World's scandium, producing
around 2 tons per year as scandium oxide, while the production of
free metallic form of scandium is in the vicinity of around 10kg
per year.

Scandium is primarily used to form aluminum-scandium alloys,
which are typically as strong as titanium, light as aluminum and
harder than ceramic compounds. These alloys contain around 0.1%
scandium.

These properties make them desirable for use in high
performance parts of airframes, as well as in the production of
military cybernetic devices.

Scandium alloys are also used in the manufacture of high
performance fuel cells.
