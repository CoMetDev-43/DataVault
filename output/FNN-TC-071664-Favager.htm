A couple of years ago a man named Henri Favager discovered some

not so secret documents on a piece of land in Saint-Fargeau, France.

These documents led to an international media circus of epic

proportions and accusations of a clandestine CIA operation known as

Project Udder Chaos.

But it was all a lie. Every...last...bit. Read on fans, read on.

French agriculturalist, Henri Favager was in fact one Maurice Baak a

down on his luck, industrial kiln operator from Antoing, Belgium.

Baak grew up in a one-bedroom house with six siblings. He dropped

out of school at the age of 13 to work in a factory. Living from

paycheck to paycheck, Baak could never get ahead and he stumbled

into a life of petty crime shortly after his father died in 2002. He

bounced around towns all over Belgium and France, occasionally

getting pinched for car theft, burglary and even arson.

According to a source close to THREAD COUNT, Baak, was allegedly

recruited by the United States Central Intelligence Agency in early

2025. His recruitment coming after Baak was nabbed in a raid by

French police for smuggling a cache of guns and drugs across the

border from Belgium. {Note: He and three French nationals were

found with two truckloads of automatic weapons, 275 pounds of

cocaine, 100 pounds of the designer drug LSZ as well as hundreds of

fake passports.}

Although the details are murky, once in the employ of the CIA, Baak

was (allegedly) involved in a number of illicit "Black Bag" operations in

France, Belgium and the Netherlands. The CIA often hiring former

criminals for these enterprises because of the convenient plausible

deniability involved.

Baak was eventually recruited for a CIA misinformation project known

as Papillion, which, from all accounts, was designed to create forged

top-secret documentation that would miraculously "fall" into enemy

hands.

On May 23, 2027 however, Baak was the star of the show. Through an

elaborate series of events, Baak under the assumed identity of one

Henri Favager, would happen to stumble upon a series of "top secret"

documents purported to have information alleging CIA

misappropriation of funds as well as a series of ridiculous claims about

weaponizing various animals such as cattle and goats.

All of it was a lie.

Baak was essentially a pay check player in a ridiculous melodrama

orchestrated by the CIA. (One source says nearly $300,000 was wired

to his account from various CIA front companies.)

What came after, an international media blitz along with a series of

U.S. congressional hearings, of course, may or may not have been

planned by the CIA.

To make this story even more interesting, nearly a decade later, Baak -

still under his assumed identity as Henri Favager - ends up careening

down a mountain in the French Alps - dead on impact. His autopsy

A dubious ending at best.
