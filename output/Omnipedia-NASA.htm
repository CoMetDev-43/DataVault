National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is the
United States governmental agency that oversees the nation's
space program.

It was established in 1958, in the wake of the dissolution of the
National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), and
remains a civilian agency.

It has been responsible for developing programs that have sent
robotic probes to all of the planetary bodies in the solar system; it
sent men to the moon in 1969 and to Mars in 2047. It has launched
space telescopes that have given us the deepest and clearest views
of the early universe ever observed.

Closer to home, it runs numerous Earth missions, monitoring
global weather and climate trends.

NASA shares much of its data with other national and
international research facilities.
