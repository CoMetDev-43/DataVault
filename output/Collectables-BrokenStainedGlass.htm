BROKEN STAINED GLASS

This is a section of brightly colored stained glass that once formed
part of the great glass roof of Cairo's Ramses Station which has
fallen into disrepair since the establishment of an Egyptian army
base on the site due to the NRC 's siege of Cairo began in 2063.

Stained glass was thought to have originated in Egypt and
Mesopotamia around 3500 BC, although it was used primarily for
beads and blown-glass bottles.

The exact additives needed to color the glass weren't fully
understood, until a Persian alchemist named Geber published a
work on the subject in the 8th century, explaining that the colors
were caused by metal oxides and salts such as Gold Chloride for
red, Iron (2) Oxide for green or Titanium for yellowish browns.

The current station was built in 1892, replacing what had been the
first train link between Cairo and Alexandria in 1856.

Modern amenities such as air conditioning and the marble floors
that give it such a palatial feel weren't added until 2011.
