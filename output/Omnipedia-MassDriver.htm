Mass Driver

A Mass Driver is a machine in which a series of electro-magnets are
switched on and off in a linear 'ripple' in order to accelerate a
payload along a track.

Dependent on the scale of the machine and the power of the
magnets used, Mass Drivers have a range of applications;
including Mag Lev trains, artillery and space launch systems.

A technical demonstrator built by NASA in 2048 showed that they
can be used to cheaply launch space craft into Low Earth Orbit.

Used as ship-based weapons for the first time by the US Navy in
the second quarter of the 21st century, Mass Driver based
weaponry presents a number of advantages over traditional
chemical based propellants.

Mass Drivers can accelerate projectiles to extreme speeds in
excess of 10 times the speed of sound, around 7600 miles per hour
- fast enough to be classified as 'hypersonic'.

A projectile moving that quickly hits so hard that no chemical
explosives are needed; the kinetic energy alone is enough to
ensure a kill-on-target.

In terms of range, a GPS guided projectile launched at that speed
is accurate out to ranges in excess of 250 miles; around 10 times
the range of conventional artillery.

As long as there is available power, and heat levels can be kept
below the operating levels of the high-temperature
superconductors used in the electromagnets, Mass Driver based
weapons can maintain a rapid rate of fire in excess of 10 rounds a
minute.

Mass Drivers have been proposed as an essential part of returning
refined Helium-3 from the Moon to the Earth in several designs
for automated Lunar mining facilities. A relatively low powered Mass
Driver would work, as it would be pushing against the Moon's
relatively weak gravitational field, beyond which the Earth's
stronger gravitational well would do the rest of the work.
