Project Stargate

Project Stargate is the collective codename for a series of
clandestine programs established by several United States
intelligence services to explore the practical military use of
psychic phenomena. The project involved a series of advanced
psychic functioning and remote viewing experiments that ran for
nearly twenty-three years before terminating in 1995.

History

In 1970, the United States Military, CIA and Defense Intelligence
Agency decided they should investigate the credibility of reports
that the Soviet Union had invested over 60 million rubles per year
into psychic research by delving into the field themselves.

In particular the reports led to the CIA's interest in the practice of
remote viewing or the alleged ability to sense unseen targets or
objects using extrasensory perception. The term remote viewing
came out of the structured protocol designed by the CIA to make
research of clairvoyance and out of body experiences sound more
scientific.

In 1984, the highly covert program came under heavy criticism
after its existence was reported by the media. This led to an
unfavorable reception by various organizations in the field of
scientific research as well as claims that the CIA was delving into
pseudoscience.

By the 1990's the project and its associated programs were
considered a failure as the majority of research and data gathering
was deemed vague, irrelevant or erroneous.

According to some leaked documentation, several of the programs
administrators may have even doctored reports in order to
prolong the programs existence.

It was reported that the total number of remote viewers involved
in the project was twenty-two at its peak and just three when the
project was terminated in 1995.
