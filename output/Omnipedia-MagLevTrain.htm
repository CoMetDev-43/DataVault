MagLev Train

A MagLev train is a mass transit system that relies on magnetic
levitation to 'float' or suspend the bodies of its carriages above the
track system, while another series of magnets, either within the
track or on the train itself push the vehicle forwards using the
same principles exploited by Mass Drivers.

Fast, comfortable, quiet and energy efficient, most commercial
train systems have now switched over to MagLev technology.

Speeds in excess of 300 miles per hour are common.

The first patent for the use of a linear motor driven train was
granted in the United States to Alfred Zehden, a German inventor
in 1905, however the first commercial system based on the
technology wasn't opened until 1984 and ran over a distance of
600 meters, connecting the Birmingham International Airport in
the United Kingdom to the nearby Birmingham International
Railway Station.
