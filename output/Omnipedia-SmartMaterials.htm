Smart Materials

Smart Materials are a class of man-made materials that are
capable of changing one or more of their properties in a
controlled, predictable fashion due to the application or presence
of a particular stimulus.

That stimulus could take many forms; to date, Smart Materials
have been produced that respond to voltage, temperature,
magnetic fields and acidity.

Simple Smart Materials may change their color, for example to
indicate the presence of particular contaminates in the
environment, or as a result of a direct electrical charge; the final
color being dictated by the voltage applied. This has applications
ranging from sensors on the battlefield alerting their users to the
presence of specific toxins, through to vanity coatings for high-end
vehicles.

Other Smart Materials are capable of changing their shape,
leading to applications such as Smart Material based cowlings on
jet and scram engines that adjust the shape of their intakes in
order to minimize the turbulence in the air-streams feeding the
engines, as they travel at faster and faster speeds.

Most modern industrial equipment as well as advanced military
armors exhibit Smart Material based 'self-healing' properties,
where the critical components of the machine or armor plates are
able to repair damage to their surfaces and internal structures
over time, requiring less maintenance and far less frequent
replacement. This process is often driven by light of specific
frequencies falling on the item in question.
