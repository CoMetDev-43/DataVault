MKULTRA

MKULTRA was the codename for one of the Central Intelligence
Agency's infamous clandestine research programs into mind
control.

MKULTRA was established in the 1950's to research and develop
chemical, biological and radiological materials capable of
controlling human behavior. The program had 149 related
projects and operations which included field operations involving
various institutions, medical facilities, universities, prisons and
research foundations across the United States.

Although covert throughout most of its existence, the program
was first brought to light in 1975 during a U.S. congressional
investigation of various CIA activities within the United States.
The investigation revealed that the CIA and Department of
Defense had conducted an extensive program of human
experimentation with chemical and biological agents in order to
influence and control human behaviors. This experimentation
included the use of many illegal drugs such as LSD and may have
even resulted in several deaths.

Much of the investigation into MKULTRA was severely hampered
as many of the files related to the program were destroyed in 1973
under the orders of the CIA director Richard Helms. Helms'
actions have often been considered part of the widespread panic
created by the Watergate scandal.

In 1977, around 20,000 documents related to MKULTRA were
released under the Freedom of Information Act, once again
triggering further investigations into the program. Although
much of the critical project information was destroyed previously
in 1973, these new files did provide a wealth of information
including insights into the financing of the MKULTRA projects.

Given the CIA's deliberate destruction of program records, its
failure to follow consent protocols by experimenting on
thousands of unwitting subjects, the unchecked nature of the
program's experiments and the full impact of MKULTRA will
likely never be known.
