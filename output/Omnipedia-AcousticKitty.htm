Acoustic Kitty

Acoustic Kitty was a clandestine CIA project that attempted to
utilize trained cats to infiltrate and spy on Soviet targets during
the Cold War.

The brainchild of the Directorate of Science & Technology, the
research and development wing of the CIA, the cats utilized in
Acoustic Kitty underwent an extensive surgical procedure to
insert microphones and antennas into their bodies so that they
could record their nearby surroundings.

The project's goal was to have these surgically altered cats placed
near Soviet targets where they would record and transmit sound
from the antennas implanted in their tails.

Missions

The first and subsequently only mission in the Acoustic Kitty
project was the infiltration of a park near the Soviet embassy in
Washington D.C. The objective was to have the cat sneak into an
area where two Soviet diplomats were sitting and eavesdrop on
their conversation. Within minutes of being released, the cat
assigned to the mission wandered into the street and was run over
by a car.

Cancellation

Ultimately, the feline operatives used in Acoustic Kitty were
difficult to train and were often distracted by their innate sense of
hunger. This made them ineffective and consequently led to the
collapse of the program.

Reports estimate that over $25 million was spent on Acoustic Kitty
and those within the CIA working close to the project considered it
a total loss. The project was officially cancelled in 1967.
