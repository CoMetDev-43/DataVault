Meta Materials

Meta-materials are man-made substances constructed from
multiple individual materials such as metals and plastics, which
exhibit 'strange properties' not usually found in nature.

They often gain these properties not because of the materials that
they are constructed from, but due to microscopic or nano -level
patterns on their surfaces, which are frequently repeating or tiled
patterns.

Meta-materials have a long history of uses in military roles, such
as in stealth applications. Early Meta-materials were used as the
basis for coatings on stealth aircraft, designed to absorb
radio-wavelengths, radiating the energy away in all directions
rather than allowing the incoming radio pulse to reflect back to its
source; thus substantially lowering the radar signature of the
coated airframe.

More advanced materials have been developed which exhibit the
'spooky' ability to bend light around the object that they coat,
essentially making the object invisible; if no photons bounce off of
the surface back to the eye, or detector of the observer, then the
object is not seen.

Other materials have been produced which selectively filter
different frequencies of light.

These have seen substantial use as coatings on windows in so
called 'smart buildings'; allowing light in the visible spectrum to
pass freely, but preventing infra-red light from entering, keeping
the structures cool.

Smart Meta-materials are a class of Smart Materials in which the
surface of the meta-material can be modified in real time; this
allows for optical camouflage that can be switched on and off, or
for dimmable glass coatings that can allow differing amounts of
light to pass through as desired.
