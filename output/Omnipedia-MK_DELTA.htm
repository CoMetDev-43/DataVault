MKDELTA

MKDELTA was a controversial, covert program developed by the

Central Intelligence Agency involving the clandestine use of Lysergic
acid diethylamide or LSD on unwitting, non-volunteer subjects
outside of the United States.

The program's goal, along with its associated domestic
counterpart MKULTRA, was to develop mind control and
interrogation techniques that could be used against foreign or
domestic opposition.

Since little is known about the MKDELTA program it is nearly
impossible to reconstruct the full scope of the project and the
procedures used. However, investigators believe LSD and other
biochemical drugs were likely used to assist in the interrogation,
coercion or deliberate incapacitation of assets valuable to the CIA
or United States.

In 2009, the program was linked to a 1951 field trial of an aerosol
based LSD delivery system that was unleashed on the
unsuspecting village of Point Saint-Esprit, France. The field trial,
initiated by the CIA, allegedly involved contaminating food and
supplies in the village with the LSD spray. The incident resulted in
thirty-two people being institutionalized for psychosis and seven
deaths.

MKDELTA's operational period is believed to have run from the
1950's until 1973 when MKULTRA and its associated projects were
officially halted by the CIA.
