Carbon

Name: Carbon

Symbol: C

Atomic Number: 6

Category: polyatomic nonmetal

The 15th most common element on Earth and 4th most abundant
in the universe, all life on Earth is based on carbon.

Found in a variety of forms (allotropes), carbon exhibits a wide
variety of different characteristics which are exploited industrially
and scientifically in an incredibly wide variety of applications.

As diamond it is the hardest naturally occurring substance, and
with the exception of the blue diamonds used in the manufacture
of semiconductors, is an excellent electrical insulator.

Industrial diamonds (accounting for around 80% of the world's
annual output) are widely used in cutting, grinding and polishing
applications due to the materials hardness. Their excellent
thermal properties are also put to use as heat sinks for high
performance, critical electronics.

At the other end of the spectrum graphite is soft as well as being a
good conductor of electricity, and is used in applications ranging
from batteries to industrial lubricants. When added in low
concentrations to iron, steel is produced.

Graphene, a form of carbon in which the atoms are arranged in a
thin, mostly transparent single atom thick sheet, exhibits some of
the strength of diamond (being around 100 times stronger than
steel), and is an excellent conductor of heat and electricity. It is
used to create advanced composite materials, for high
performance batteries, solar panels, micro-electronics, surgical
implants and a wide variety of other applications.

Fullerenes; a class of carbons where the atoms are arranged to
form spheres, or extruded to form 'nanotubes' revolutionized a
number of fields, ranging from pharmaceuticals,
superconductors, super-capacitors, medical implants, electronics
and ballistic armor over the 50 years following their discovery in
1985.
