Cochlear Implant

The Cochlear Implant is a surgically implanted device designed to
restore hearing in people suffering from specific forms of deafness.

The first Cochlear Implants were first successfully tested in 1977,
although it wasn't until the device's capabilities had improved
throughout the 80's and 90's that the implants usage became wide
spread.

These ' first and second phase' devices allowed deaf people who
had suffered either damage or malformation in either the
tympanum, bones of the inner ear or of the hairs in the cochlear
itself to hear.

The implanted portion of the device threads a string of electrodes
into the snail-like structure of the cochlear, providing direct
electrical stimulus of the neurons of the inner hair cells, allowing
sounds to be perceived by the recipient.

The user wears a microphone and a small processing device,
which filters, picks out and enhances relevant sounds such as
speech, before converting them into electrical signals that are
communicated to the implanted section through the skull via
induction.

While sounds perceived through Cochlear Implants are not
'perfect', numerous videos can be found online that show how
profound the experience can be when the devices are switched on
for the first time.

As technology has continued to improve, Cochlear Implants have
moved away from stimulating the hair cells, to providing direct
stimulation to the auditory nerve itself - providing recipients with
far higher quality results as well as expanding the number of
conditions that can be treated.

The most recent generations of the technology are able to push
signals directly into Heshchl's gyrus; part of the primary auditory
cortex in the brain itself, meaning that deafness resulting from
any causes affecting the primary auditory pathway up to that
point can be rectified.

Cochlear Implants have changed hundreds of thousands of lives.
The device can be considered the first true Cybernetic Implant.
