Second World War

The Second World War, also known as World War II (WWII or
WW2), was a global conflict that took place between 1939 and
1945. The war involved the majority of the world's nations
eventually forming two opposing military alliances: the Allies and
the Axis.

It was the largest and deadliest war in history, involving more
than 100 million people from over 30 countries.

The chief participants threw their full economic, industrial and
scientific capabilities behind the war effort, removing the
distinction between civilian and military resources.

While the Empire of Japan declared war against the Republic of
China in 1937, the war is considered to have begun with
Germany's invasion of Poland in 1939 and the subsequent
declarations of war on Germany by France and the United
Kingdom.

The United States did not join the conflict until the Japanese
bombed Pearl Harbor on December 7th 1941.

The war brought the deaths of many civilians, including the
Holocaust (which killed approximately 11 million people) and the
strategic bombing of industrial and civilian centers (during which
approximately one million people were killed, including the first
use of two nuclear weapons in combat), an estimated 60 million
lives were lost as a result of World War II.

It was the most destructive and deadly conflict in human history.
