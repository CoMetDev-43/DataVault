Allied Drone Defense Act

The US Allied Drone Defense Act was passed into law in 2026 in
the wake of Raul Menendez' compromise of the US military's
command and control networks in 2025 that allowed him to
commission acts of terror on a grand scale, including the attempt
on the lives of the leaders of the G8 nations in Los Angeles using a
swarm of US military drones.

Under the provisions of the bill, all military networks were to be
hardened, including the addition of triple and quadruple fail-safes
on all critical network nodes in the infrastructure, allowing them
to be isolated from the network in the event of a breach.

New mesh networking technologies would ensure that command
and control would remain in place, even if high numbers of nodes
had to be ditched following massive breaches in security.

Additionally, all US military networks were to be constantly tested
for vulnerabilities by a newly formed and independently run
cyber-warfare division. This division was also to be responsible for
the full auditing of all computer code submitted by defense
companies for use on systems used by the United States military
forces.

The US would extend their new command and control protocols
to all of their NATO and other allies; to all nations with whom the
US conducted joint military exercises.

The second part of the Allied Drone Defense Act laid out minimum
annual budgets on deployable defenses for both military and
civilian targets; to be spent on improving Ballistic Missile Defense
systems and increasing the efficacy of the nascent Directed Energy
Air Defenses that were in early deployment at that time.

Initial funding for the bill was provided by the forced
asset-striping of Menendez' shell company, the Tacitus
Corporation, which was conducted under legislation that provides
for the forfeiture of terrorist finance sources.
