Caseless Liquid Propellant

Most modern artillery weapons that don't use mass-driver
technology are based on Caseless Liquid Propellant systems.

The magazine of the weapon contains warheads, often in a
number of configurations for defeating different target types,
which are automatically selected and loaded into the breach of the
weapon by automatic robotic loading systems.

Meanwhile, a two-part liquid explosive that has been stored in
separate tanks is mixed together and injected into a combustion
chamber behind the loaded round moments before the weapon
fires.

The quantity of liquid propellant used per shot varies, dependent
on the targets range and the intended trajectory of the shot.

This system offers a number of advantages over conventional
cased-shell systems; firstly the rounds are more compact, allowing
higher capacity magazines to be deployed in the field.

Secondly the explosive propellants components are inert until
mixed; meaning that in the event of the weapons destruction
there is little risk of secondary explosions causing collateral
damage.
