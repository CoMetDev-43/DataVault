Augmented Reality

Augmented Reality (AR) is a term applied to an enhanced optical
system that overlays additional contextual information over the
environment around a user.

Using some form of display device, such as smart glasses, smart
lenses or projection onto a transparent surface in front of the
users face, AR can add information ranging from user reviews of a
product that the user is looking at, to speed and relative distance
information of objects moving around them.

In architectural and construction environments AR can allow
people touring a construction site to see parts of a building, as yet
unbuilt, overlaid in their final locations.

Surgeons enjoy overlays of real-time medical scans, allowing them
to see the structures that they're operating on in greater detail
than standard medical camera systems allow.

Eye-tracking technology can be used to ensure that the
information presented to the user is contextually correct, and to
avoid overwhelming the visual field with clutter.

In training applications, the controls of a vehicle or machine can
be highlighted in the correct order of operation, with information
ranging from written information on use, to more 'instantly
useful' advanced 3 dimensional renderings of the controls being
moved to their desired positions.

Originally conceptualized by American Author L. Frank Baum (b.
1856 – d. 1919), writer of popular children's book 'The Wonderful
Wizard of Oz' as a pair of spectacles that overlay data onto real life,
Baum referred to the system as a 'Character Marker' that could
highlight people of interest.

The term AR was first coined in 1990, while the first actual
working AR system as we would recognize it was developed by the
US Air Force Research Laboratory in 1992.

While early AR prototype systems began to appear in the 2010's,
commercial non-military applications of the technology began to
become commonplace in the early 2020's.
