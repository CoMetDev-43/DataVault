Techie in the Shell

1/17/60

It was a good day today. It was a bad day today. It was an amazing day.

Immersion; they tell you that it's going to be a bit like virtual reality, but that's
like telling someone that virtual reality is a bit like watching TV.

You close your eyes, or just tell the DNI system that you want to 'blink', which
is like setting the signal input on your TV or receiver system - but for your
eyes.

Then you're somewhere else.

The Immersion they selected for me was a meadow under blue, cloudless
skies. Wild flowers in an array of dazzling colors bloomed everywhere, and
bees buzzed industrially from synthetic bloom to synthetic bloom, filling the
air around me with the sound of their droning.

That's when I looked around, and it became a bad day.

Some kind of calibration error to do with the rate of my head rotation I'm
told...

Turns out that when your eyes are telling you one thing, and your inner ear
isn't telling you the exact same thing you get sick.

Real sick - 0/5 do not recommend.

I felt awful, and as the Immersion melted away, dumping me back into the
confines of the medical facility I began to realize I'd actually been sick - I
hadn't noticed the physical sensation as everything had been being
over-ridden by the Immersion which was set not to blend in my own actual
physical sensations.

This is powerful technology, people.

After tidying myself up, a quick shower and a bunch of assurances that I
wanted to go again- they tweaked a few parameters on my DNI system
remotely - that spine tingling sensation again - and wham - back to the
meadow.

After a few cautious head turns to verify that they'd gotten it right, I was on
my feet and running. On my bare feet.

A few seconds previously I'd been wearing sneakers, and now I could feel the
grass bending under foot, and lightly springing back up as I ran.

After a few short, joyful minutes it was over, and I was being asked to rest.

I can't explain the sense of freedom that this technology engenders in me. I
can reach beyond the confines of my reach- of my body. I can't express the
gratitude I feel towards the Coalescence Corporation for selecting me for
this Voyager Program. I've barely scratched the surface of what this
technology can do, and I already know that I'm not the same man that I was
when I arrived here 2 short weeks ago. When was the last time you could say
that about a gadget you'd purchased?

I can't imagine what new wonders tomorrow might bring.
