Cold War

The Cold War (1947 - 1991) was a global period of political and
military tension between the Eastern Bloc (the Soviet Union and
its Warsaw Pact allies) and the West (the United States and NATO )

The Cold War as was fought along economic and philosophical
lines; one side espousing Marxist-Leninist principles along with
having a single ruling political party, while the other followed
democratic capitalist principles. Both sides saw each other as an
existential threat to each-other's way of life.

The 44 year long conflict saw no direct military engagements
between each side, as both sides were committed a nuclear arms
race, which at peak levels saw the Soviet Union's stockpiles of
nuclear warheads reach an estimated 40,000 in 1988, which US
levels peaked at around 32,000 in the late 1960's.

These weapons, each up to 2,500 times more powerful than the
weapons dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki at the end of the
Pacific campaign of the second world war, could have been
delivered by ICBM and by fleets of long-range strategic bombers,
had the capacity to destroy the Earth many times over.

Faced with the prospect of mutually assured destruction
guaranteed by their nuclear doctrines, the major players engaged
in political and diplomatic brinkmanship, each daring the other to
blink. On a number of occasions it is estimated that the world was
within hours of thermonuclear destruction.

Throughout the 1950's and 1960's the two sides instead engaged in
a number of 'proxy wars', in which they attempted to gain
influence, while containing each other's allies and interests
around the world; examples of which include the Korean War, and
later Vietnam.

Following a series of breakaways by former soviet republics in the
mid 80's, and the reunification of East and West Germany in 1989,
the end of the Cold War was marked on the 25th of December
1991, when the Soviet Union formally dissolved.
