An Argument for Battlefield Robots

If there's one thing that liberal media seems to be beating their drums about
these days it's the fact that we're seemingly moments away from the
battlefield deployment of so called "Killer Robots" by the United States
military. Along with those blistering drum beats is, of course, the skewed
perception that we will have jackbooted robotic battle units marching down
Pennsylvania Avenue by next Tuesday if we let this "travesty" occur.

I think we can all agree that automated killing machines mowing down
humans is not something we're anticipating. Everyone inside and out of this
debate agrees that a lot of questions need to be raised about sending
robots into battle, from basic - who and how will these robots operate
amongst our armed forces, to complex - should robots have the right to pull
the trigger and will they be morally capable of making the right decision?

As the debate rages amongst our policy makers, the military industrial
manufacturing community and their human rights opposition ( Humana
Super Machina) we will inevitably come to a conclusion that will either
satiate everyone or perhaps no one at all.

Whatever the outcome of that debate, I'm here to tell you that we need
these robotic units in order to help restore our national pride and remove
this country from the precipice of destruction.

I know this might not be a popular point of view, but hear me out.

Flip on your television or browse any popular news site and you'll see a
myriad of articles about our troops being shipped to every Winslow Accord
hotspot around the world. Although our President would deny it, we're
engaging in more battles today than the whole of World War II.

While the American military footprint expands, albeit with a much more
morally ambiguous mission than killing axis soldiers in 1941 or at least that's
what the media would have you believe, we continue to push our troops into
harm's way while we have this advanced technology sitting on the sidelines.

Until 2025, when our blissful ignorance was interrupted by Raul Menendez,
we were happy to send drones into combat. One might have concluded that
we had more drones in the sky at the time then stars. However, we all know
the outcome of that attack and why our mighty air force turned into squawkin'
chicken hawks.

So we've invested our military dollars into bolstering ground troops, training
and of course advanced technology. But, why aren't we using all of this
equipment to help save the lives of our own military personnel?

The media is concerned about battlefield robots having "unchecked lethal
force." The simple fact of the matter is that robots will always receive their
commands from a human, whether it's an operating system, a program or a
battlefield command.

Robots do what we ask of them, they don't operate independent of humans.

It's not a question of robots with morals, but rather the humans who control
the robots and whether they have morals.

I can tell you that if this type of technology falls into our enemies hands, they
will not think twice about unleashing hell on our troops.

And so why shouldn't we be the first to market? Why not save American lives
while we still can?
