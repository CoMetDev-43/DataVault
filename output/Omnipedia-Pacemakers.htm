Pacemakers

A Pacemaker is an electronic device, which when implanted takes
over the regulation of the recipients heart beat by directly
electrically stimulating the muscles of the heart with electrodes.

While the practical research and experiments that lead to the
creation of the first Pacemaker dates back to 1889, the operation to
implant the first true device was carried out in Sweden in 1958.

While the working principles have remained the same, the device
has undergone radical miniaturization, going from a large device
implanted in the abdominal cavity to an intracardial system the
size of a pill, which can be inserted directly into the heart after
being threaded up the femoral artery - a minimally invasive
technique requiring almost no recovery time.

With the exception of purely mechanical implants such as
artificial hip and knee joints, the Pacemaker is the commonest
electronic implant, responsible for the extension of both quality
and duration of life in tens of millions of recipients world-wide.
