CDP

The CDP ( Common Defense Pact ) is a Mutual Defense Treaty
comprising a substantial number of European Nations as well as
representatives from the Middle East, Sub Saharan Africa and
South America.

The full list of CDP Signatory Nations may be found elsewhere.

Inception

The CDP owes its inception to the economic and political turmoil
that followed in the wake of the Collapse of the European Union as
well as to rising global competition over dwindling oil supplies,
access to limited resources such as Rare Earth Elements used by
high-tech industries and protectionist policies taken by nations
towards their growing private space launch sectors.

After the withdrawal of France and Germany from the European
Union and the collapse of the Euro, Poland became the first ex-EU
to state to sign new trade treaties with Russia.

The Warsaw Purchase, signed in 2025 would return Poland to
solvency and offered energy security in return for commitments
on political issues and exclusivity guarantees on purchases of
natural gas and other energy exports from Russia; it would form
the template for the treaties that would follow.

Expansion and a changing of the European Guard

Over the course of 2026 first Hungary, then Romania and finally
the Czech Republic would forge their own agreements with
Russia; each issuing fiery diplomatic releases slamming the 'EU
Rich Clique' for abandoning the economically smaller members of
Europe when 'things got tough'.

2027 brought a second wave of expansion, drawing Bulgaria,
Finland, Kazakhstan, Greece and Ukraine into the diplomatic fold.
Russia drafted the Trans-European Treaty formalizing the
Russian/European Super-Block of Nations.

In 2028, internally log-jammed by the increasing weight of
opinion from its member-nations who also belonged to the
Trans-European Treaty, NATO formally disbanded, no longer able
to adapt to the changing diplomatic picture of Europe; Algeria,
Belarus, Cuba, Estonia, Latvia and Slovakia would join over the
course of the rest of the year.

Growing Military Confidence

Military tensions escalated dangerously in 2029, when Russia;
keen to demonstrate that its aging ICBM program still represented
a credible deterrent despite the USA's increasingly sophisticated BMD
capabilities, knocked out an aging telecommunications satellite
belonging to the US using a repurposed ICBM.

Two days later a US Aegis Cruiser destroyed a commercial Russian
rocket carrying 3 GLONASS satellites during its launch phase. The
US would claim that their systems incorrectly identified the rocket
as an ICBM launch, initiating an automatic interception.

2030 saw the infant trade-and-energy hegemony substantially
break free of the constraints of Continental Europe, with Angola,
Nigeria and Pakistan signing on; the African nations brought with
them substantial Petroleum production might, with Pakistan
contributing mineral wealth in return for assurances on its
manufacturing sectors which were starting to suffer due to global
movement towards local manufacture.

In 2031 the Lomonosov Incident would draw the USA and Russia
into conflict again, with the destruction of a US Research Ship by a
Russian Navy Vessel in contested waters highlighting the two sides
increasing competition over under sea mineral and oil rights.

European Diplomatic Coup

In a move that came as a surprise to Western European Observers,
Germany would sign the Trans-European Treaty in 2032, bringing
with it its own substantial industrial and fiscal base, as well as
pledges of membership from Austria and Italy.

Birth of the Common Defense Pact

In 2036 after the first historic naval maneuvers conducted in the
East China Sea involving US, Chinese and Japanese vessels drew
condemnation from Russia, Iran & Pakistan, and against the
backdrop of a worsening global Recession of the Poorest, which
gripped nations whose manufacturing based economies were
suffering due to local manufacture, Iran signed the
Trans-European Treaty.

At the ceremony, Iranian representatives proposed that the
existing treaty be upgraded to a full Mutual Defense Treaty. In the
highly stage managed scenes that followed, a vote passed (with
only Germany abstaining) and the CDP was born.

Under the terms of the treaty, an attack on one nation would be
seen as an attack on all nations. Nations would keep their
sovereign status, but would also contribute funds annually for the
training and supply of their allies' forces.

In the months that followed, Germany would withdraw from the

CDP.

For Every Action, Reaction

In 2037 in direct retaliation for the creation of the CDP, the US
Secretary of State drafted the self-named Winslow Accords,
creating a CDP facing Mutual Defense Treaty extending to all US
allies.

Further Expansion

2037 also saw further Middle Eastern expansion for the CDP, with
Libya and Syria being granted membership.

After lengthy negotiation with Kenya in 2039 over mining rights
for vast new reserves of Rare Earth Elements discovered there,
Kenya would formally join the CDP ; bringing in the rest of her Nile
River Coalition partners in 2040.

As a result of internal political changes, Italy would leave the CDP
in 2041 allying itself with the Winslow Accord.

Yemen became a signatory in 2051, with Somalia following suit in
2052.
