Tiger II

The Tiger II was a German tank used during World War II. It was
designed in 1943 and saw service during the last 2 years of the war,
between 1944 and 1945. 492 were manufactured.

Designed by Henschel & Son, it combined the heavy armor of the
Tiger I with the sloped profile of the Panther medium tank.

Weighing in at around 70 metric tons and crewed by a team of 5
(Commander, gunner, loader, radio operator and driver), its 690
horse-power V-12 gasoline engine could reach a maximum road
speed of 26 miles an hour, peaking at around 13 miles an hour
when travelling across country.

Carrying 860 liters of fuel, it had an operational range of around
100 miles.

The Tiger II's main armament was an 8.8cm KwK 43 turret, for
which it carried a magazine of 86 rounds. It was backed up by two
7.92mm MG34 machine guns, each with around a 6000 round
capacity.

Despite being plagued by a number of reliability issues, the Tiger II
was a formidable weapons platform. Its main turret could destroy
any armored fighting vehicles deployed by the allied forces
beyond the listed effective range of their cannons.

The Tiger II's front armor was impervious to the majority of allied
tank or artillery fire beyond a range of 1000 meters, while its
cannon could defeat incoming armor at ranges of greater than
2500 meters.
