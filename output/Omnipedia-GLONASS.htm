GLONASS

GLONASS, or "Global Navigation Satellite System" is Russia's
satellite navigation system, and is run by the Russian Aerospace
Defense Forces.

It is an alternate system to the US run Global Positioning System
(GPS), and has global coverage and offers a similar degree of
precision.

The first satellite was launched in 1982, with the full constellation
of 24 active satellites coming online in 1995.

Suffering from budgetary issues, the system in orbit would not
become operational, and numerous satellites would be lost.

In 2001 funding for the system was made a priority, and in 2010
the system achieved full orbital coverage of Russian territory with
global coverage following in 2011.

The system came on line for general usage globally in December
2015.

As with any satellite system intended to remain in service
permanently, members of the constellation are frequently retired
as they run out of station-keeping fuel, and are allowed to burn up
in the atmosphere. Launches continue at the rate of 2 - 3 a year
ensuring uninterrupted service.
