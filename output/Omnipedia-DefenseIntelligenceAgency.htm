Defense Intelligence Agency

The Defense Intelligence Agency or DIA, is one of the principal
members of the United States intelligence community. Operating
in conjunction with the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD), the DIA
is responsible for the collection and analysis of the myriad of
foreign intelligence data utilized by U.S. policymakers.

Formed in 1961, the DIA's main goal was to help integrate and
disseminate information originating from the U.S. Department of
Defense to various intelligence units. The agency was involved in
a continuous task of collecting, processing and analyzing this
information as well creating efficiencies in DoD tasks. Initially,
many within the U.S. intelligence community felt the formation of
the DIA was an encroachment on their territory.

During the 1970's the DIA consolidated its functions, grew its
reputation and became increasingly recognized as a valuable part
of the intelligence community. This was achieved through
successful operations in the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

In the 1980's the DIA was critical in identifying the strength and
capabilities of the Soviet military forces with some of the first
white papers on the subject. During this time, the Agency also
shifted its focus to key issues like changes within the Soviet Union,
counter narcotics, monitoring terrorist groups and identifying
illegal arm shipments.

By 2012, the agency's focus shifted to intelligence collection and
analysis of data around nuclear proliferation in developing
countries and adversaries like North Korea.

Today, the DIA works in conjunction with various branches of the
United States intelligence community and has a myriad of
activities that focus on military intelligence concerns that other
agencies have been unable to manage due to lack of personnel or
resources.
