Warsaw Pact

The Warsaw Pact was a Mutual Defense Treaty formed in 1955 in
reaction to the admittance of Western Germany into NATO. The

Soviet Union had applied to join NATO in 1954, but their
application was rejected.

The signatory nations of the Warsaw Pact were:

· People's Republic of Albania

· People's Republic of Bulgaria

· Czechoslovak Republic

· German Democratic Republic

· People's Republic of Hungary

· People's Republic of Poland

· People's Republic of Romania

· Union of Soviet Socialist Republics ( Soviet Union )

The Warsaw Pact nations and those of NATO would draft policies
intended to contain each other in Europe for the entire duration
of the Cold War, but would never directly wage war against one
another.

In 1965, the People's Republic of Hungary announced its
withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact ; in response, Soviet troops
entered the country and toppled the government.

Dissolution

The People's Republic of Albania formally withdrew from the Warsaw
Pact after 13 years in 1968.

During the late 1980s the Communist Government of the Soviet
Union began implementing wide spread reforms that promoted
greater freedoms and openness; between 1989 and 1991, the
remaining member nations all elected non-communist
governments.

1990 was a year of great change for the pact; The People's Republic
of Poland would withdraw in January, while the German
Democratic Republic would leave in September, prior to the
re-unification of Germany on the 3rd of October 1990.

In Prague on the 25th of February 1991, the pact was officially
dissolved.

In March 1999, The Czech Republic, Hungary and Poland officially
joined NATO. Bulgaria, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Romania and
Slovakia followed in March 2004. Albania joined in April 2009.
