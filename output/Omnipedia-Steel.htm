Steel

Steel is the name given to alloys of Iron and Carbon, which have
been used world-wide in all manners of construction since newly
discovered processes allowing it to be produced cheaply came
online in the mid-19th century; from the frames of large
structures, to railways and road-surfaces.

Prized for its low cost and high tensile strength to weight ratio - steel
was a ubiquitous raw material for over a century.

At its peak rate of production, global steel production was over 1.3
billion tons annually; it has since lost ground, especially in
extreme-performance roles to a number of more modern
composite materials that offer better weight/strength properties.

The carbon content of steel maxes out at about 2% of its weight; the
higher the carbon content, the stronger and more brittle the steel.

Additional metals such as chromium, molybdenum, niobium or

titanium may be added to the steel, dependent on the properties
desired from the resulting alloy.
