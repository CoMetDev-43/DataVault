Cybernetic Implants

Formally a cybernetic implant is any electronically controlled
system implanted into the body, which is controlled directly by, or
provides input directly to the nervous system.

For example, a cybernetic limb differs from a standard mechanical
or robotic prosthetic limb by virtue of being grafted directly onto
the body, using titanium posts embedded directly into the existent
joint structure. It is directly controlled by impulses from the
original motivating nerves, or by a previously implanted Direct
Neural Interface (DNI) system.

Cybernetic enhancements have already proved controversial in
the world of professional sports.

Other classes of cybernetic implant in concert with a DNI system,
look to enhance a recipients cognitive abilities rather than their
physical ones, for example a Computer Assisted Memory (CAM)
system that allows for the instant review of everything that's been
seen or heard, as well as the recall of any manually loaded digital
information, or a co-processor designed to present the
visualization of complex mathematical functions to a user
working in a technical or scientific field.

Systems designed for implantation into the body are coated with
special bio-compatible coatings, which prevent the host's immune
systems from rejecting them.

The word "Cybernetic" is derived from a Greek word meaning "To
govern, control or steer."

The first true Cybernetic implant was the Cochlear Implant ; a
device that can allow people with certain classes of deafness to
hear which was first tested successfully in 1977.

It can be argued that the pacemaker is also a Cybernetic device, as
it is an implanted system that takes over the regulation of the
recipients heart rate from the heart's natural pacemaker.
