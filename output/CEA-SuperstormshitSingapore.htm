SUPERSTORMS HIT SINGAPORE

11/03/58 - SINGAPORE

Clean up operations are underway in Singapore today as the islands'
residents struggle to get to grips with the devastation left in the wake of
Hurricane Erin, which made land-fall last night as a category 3 storm.

The "perfect storm" brought with it a storm tide, a storm surge coinciding
with high tide that arrived on the coast 16 feet higher than mean sea level,
overcoming coastal defenses and driving inland as an inexorable wall of
water more than 6 feet high.

In scenes more commonly associated with the aftermath of tsunamis,
workers are clearing debris from the streets and in some areas using cranes
to lift boats that have been beached many miles inland.

At this time 200 people are reported either missing or dead. Peak wind
intensity was measured at 117 mph.

Many of those who are unaccounted for are feared to have been swept out
to sea by the surging tide.

A meteorologist with the Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS) said,
"We've never seen a surge of this intensity before. The models predicted it
was always going to be a 'when' rather than an 'if' this was going to occur.
Sea levels have risen 18 inches from their levels at the start of the century,
partly as a result of the thermal expansion of the warming oceans and
partly due to the run off from the receding ice caps in the polar-regions. Our
tidal defenses aren't up to the task."

"We should expect this sort of thing to happen with increasing frequency",
he added.
