Iron

Name: Iron

Symbol: Fe

Atomic Number: 26

Category: transition metal

Iron is by mass the most common element on Earth. Pure iron is a
relatively soft metal; however it gains hardness from impurities,
in particular from carbon, which at proportions below 2% gives

steel.

Iron has been used since around 1200BC, and has many uses; from
the thermite reaction used in welding to forming complex
catalysts used in organic synthesis, as well as it's more obvious
macro-scale applications in construction and manufacturing.

Iron also plays a pivotal role in biology; from the earliest forms of
single cellular life, to humans, to the most advanced artificially
engineered organisms used in the synthesis of fuels and the
clean-up of hazardous waste, iron based proteins play their part.

All vertebrates (animals with back-bones), with the exception of a
single family of perciform fish which have no red blood cells, use
hemoglobin, an iron containing protein, to exchange carbon
dioxide for oxygen in their lungs or gills and to transport it
around the rest of their bodies. It is the iron in hemoglobin that
gives blood its red color.
