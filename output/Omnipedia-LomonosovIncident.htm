Lomonosov Incident

The Lomonosov Incident was one of many international incidents
that occurred following the collapse of the European Union in
2025, and the dissolution of NATO in 2028 that further ratcheted
up the tension between the United States and Russia.

On the morning of July 11th 2031, the US 'research ship' the USS
Pleasant was sunk with the loss of all hands by the Russian Navy
while it was operating in contested waters in the Arctic Ocean.

The United States claimed that the USS Pleasant had been
carrying out measurements of deep sea temperatures as part of
their routine ongoing data collection efforts monitoring the rate of

Climate Change.

The Russians stated that the USS Pleasant was in fact a
prospecting vessel, which was exploring the mineral wealth of the
Lomonsov Ridge; an 1800km long sub-sea mountain range that
runs from the Eastern Siberian Ridge off of the Russian Coast, to
the Lincoln Shelf which stretches from the coast of Greenland to
the Beaufort shelf off of Canada.

The Lomonsov Ridge was discovered in 1948 by Russia, and is
named for Russian mathematician Mikhail Lomonosov. It has
been the center of numerous territorial claims throughout the
21st Century, including those of Russia, Denmark and Canada.

Wreckage recovered from the ocean floor included a number of
remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), which the Russians claim were
equipped with deep core sampling equipment.

These findings were backed up by an independent panel of
enquiry.
