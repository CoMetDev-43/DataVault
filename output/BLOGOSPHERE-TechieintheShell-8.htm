Techie in the Shell

1/16/60

This is amazing!

I don't know what else to tell you, other than THIS IS FREAKING AMAZING.

I don't know why I had any second thoughts. The nerves, the post-operative
pain - it was all worthwhile.

Yesterday I was allowed out of bed for the first time, and was told to make
my way down to the cafeteria to get myself a snack.

I started to complain that I didn't know the layout of the facility, when the
doctors/technicians looking after me punched a couple of keys into a
terminal, and I felt the sensation on my spine that I'm starting to associate
with incoming data.

Then clear as day, overlaid over my vision, an arrow on the ground showing
me where I had to go.

Now I'm no stranger to using virtual or augmented reality setups- but this
has a completely different look and feel; the arrow on the floor? It looks like
part of the environment - not a simple AR style overlay. It's rock solid. It
doesn't swim or float or look even slightly out of place.

As I walked to the cafeteria, I started seeing names, job titles and minor
biography information moving next to the people I was passing.

It was never cluttered - I guess the system can tell who I'm paying attention
to, and who I'm not. There's a whole layer of 'intention intuition' going on here
that I've never experienced before. It's completely natural. It's completely a
part of me.

I could have used not knowing about the processed fat content of that
muffin.

There are a few other volunteer members of the Voyager program here. Our
ID cards are all green edged, and everyone is very polite to us.

I've noticed there are quite a few people here that my DNI system isn't giving
me any information on. Not even a name. I'm guessing that the military are
paying very close attention to all aspects of this program.

There's something effortless about this technology. The 'special sauce' of the
Coalescence Corporation's DNI system is that it learns you, as you learn it. I
can feel it happening. Each time I use something, it all becomes just a little
bit more second-nature.

I'm already quite the expert at operating the smart-features of my hospital
room; it's always the right brightness, the right temperature. It hardly takes a
second thought.

Spent the rest of the day chilling while the technicians carried out optical
and auditory calibration tests - tomorrow I get to try an Immersion for the
first time!
