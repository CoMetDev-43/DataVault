Virtual Reality

A VIRTUAL REALITY (VR) system presents its user with a 3
dimensional computer generated simulated environment,
utilizing some kind of wearable stereoscopic display, either in the
form of a headset, a pair of goggles or through some kind of retinal
projection technology.

Headsets and goggles usually incorporate speakers so positional
audio can be used to enhance the users VR experience.

Additionally the experience can be made more immersive through
the use of haptic technologies; feedback systems that simulate
forces on the user meant to represent their interactions with the
world around them.

The term " virtual reality " was first used by French director
Antonin Artaud (b. 1884 d. 1968) in his 1938 book "The Theatre
and its double", in which he likened the environment created for
an audience by a theatre as being a " virtual reality ".

VR has seen wide usage in diverse applications from archaeology,
education, city layout and video gaming. It remained in the
purview of academia, industry and the military until the late
2010s, when drops in the price of manufacturing the headsets and
displays that they use, as well as the cost of the powerful graphics
hardware needed to render multiple instances of compelling
environments fell to the point that a consumer market would
bear; uptake amongst gaming enthusiasts was rapid.

While rapidly advancing graphical capabilities mean that the
environments presented by VR have become more and more
realistic, the users' interactions with it have remained 'artificial';
for example players having to navigate a game space using an
input device like a control pad or mouse.
