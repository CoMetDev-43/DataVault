Korean War

The Korean War (1950 - 1953) was a Cold War conflict fought
between North and South Korea.

Prior to the Second World War, a unified Korea had been ruled by
Japan.

In August of 1945 supported by their allies of the time, the United
States, the Soviet Union declared war on Japan and occupied what
would become North Korea.

The United States then sent its forces into South Korea. Both sides
set up governments in their jurisdictions, each of which claimed
to the legitimate government of the entire country.

On the 25th of June 1950, the uneasy truce was shattered when
North Korean forces, supported by China and the Soviet Union
invaded the South.

The United Nations recognized the act as an invasion and called
for a ceasefire. When no ceasefire was forthcoming, they
dispatched a United Nations taskforce, headed by the United
States and composed of troops and materiel from 21 different
countries to the defense of South Korea.

After a 3 year war of attrition, which saw the first air-to-air combat
between jet aircraft in the history of armed conflict, an armistice
was signed, creating a demilitarized zone between the two nations.

It's estimated that on both sides of the war over 1.2 million
military personnel were killed, while over 1.6 million civilians
perished.
