Aluminum

Name: Aluminum (or Aluminium)

Symbol: Al

Atomic Number: 13

Category: post-transition metal

Despite its salts having been used widely in the ancient world to
set dyes in dying processes and in first aid applications, the base
metal was first produced in an impure form by Danish physicist
and chemist Hans Christian Oersted in 1825.

The method used was improved upon by German chemist
Friedrich Wöhler in 1827, who is credited with isolating
aluminum.

The metal was so difficult to produce that throughout the 19th
Century pure aluminum was more valuable ounce for ounce than gold
.

Modern methods are highly efficient, allowing it to be produced
cheaply from the commonly occurring ore bauxite.

It is a soft, durable, light, malleable, silvery gray non-magnetic
metal. Aluminum is a good conductor of heat and electricity, and
is easily cast and machined. Further, it exhibits excellent
resistance to corrosion. Aluminum is highly recyclable.

Aluminum is the second most widely used metal, behind iron and
finds uses in a wide variety of applications; from packaging
through to paints and solid rocket fuels.

The world's largest producers of aluminum are China, Russia,
Canada and the USA. Australia is the world's largest supplier of the
ore bauxite.
