Panther Tank

The Panther was a medium tank, designed by MAN in 1942, and
deployed by the Germans during the Second World War between
1943 and 1945. It was used by the British army, post war, between
1946 and 1949. All in all, around 6000 units were manufactured.

The tank was designed in such a way as to address raw materials
shortages that were being experienced by the Germans during the
final stages of the war.

It weighed approximately 45 tons, and was crewed b y a team of 5
(Gunner, loader, driver, radio-operator/machine gunner and
commander). Its V-12 gasoline engine produced 690 horse-power,
had a top speed of 34 miles an hour and a range of 160 miles from
a fuel load of 720 liters.

While the tank commanded the respect of the forces that opposed
it, it was rushed into service resulting in the earlier models
experiencing a number of technical problems and a high
break-down rate.

The Panther's primary gun was a 7.5 cm canon developed and
built by Rheinmetall-Borsig, 5.25 meters in length. It had a
maximum range of 10km.

Additionally the Panther was equipped with a pair of 7.92mm MG-34
machine guns.
