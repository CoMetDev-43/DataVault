Yttrium

Name: Yttrium

Symbol: Y

Atomic Number: 39

Category: transition metal

First identified by German chemist Friedrich Wohler in 1828, and
named Yttrium for the mineral in which it was found, ytterbite;
itself named after the Swedish village Ytterby outside which the
mineral was first found by Carl Alex Arrhenius in 1787, Yttrium is
classified as a rare earth element and has numerous important
applications in manufacturing and high tech industries.

Yttrium is used in the manufacture of Light Emitting Diodes
(LEDs), as well as in the production of various types of synthetic
garnets. Those garnets have applications ranging from the purely
decorative to the Yttrium Aluminum Garnets used to create YAG
lasers; high powered lasers often used medically, and also found in
metal cutting machines and rapid prototyping systems.

It is added to glass and ceramic formulas to enhance hardness and
to impart shock resistance, finding uses in camera lenses and
ceramic armor plating.

Additionally certain yttrium compounds exhibit super-conductive
properties; in particular yttrium barium copper oxide is known to
super-conduct above the boiling point of liquid nitrogen, making
it a relatively high temperature superconductor.

Yttrium based super conductors can be found at the heart of much
modern military technology, including the power systems used by

Directed Energy Air Defense systems as well as in the drive
systems of modern MagLev Train systems.
