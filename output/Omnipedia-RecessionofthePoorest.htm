Recession of the Poorest

The Recession of the Poorest (2031 - 2050) was the name given to a
global recession that hit the world's largest developing-nation
manufacturing based economies, as the developed nations began
their transition to local manufacture and away from imports.

Tariffs on fuel used by ocean going freighters rose sharply as some
of the worst climate change predictions began to come true with
the global temperature rose, driving extreme weather conditions
and rising sea levels.

As the cost of foreign imports began to spiral, local manufacture
became the economically obvious choice for the technologically
developed countries that could afford it; those that lacked a
well-developed internal consumption based market-place of their
own suffered the brunt of the economic downturn.

Even economic behemoths like China felt a pinch, as orders from
the West sharply fell off.

Additionally as the global shipping market contracted - now
mostly handling raw materials, rather than diverse, finished
goods, the World's major trade hubs began to collapse; from
Singapore and Shanghai, to Rotterdam and Hamburg, the volume
of traffic in 2045 fell below that of 2000.
