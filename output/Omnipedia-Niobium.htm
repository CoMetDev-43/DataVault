Niobium

Name: Niobium

Symbol: Nb

Atomic Number: 41

Category: transition metal

Discovered in 1801 by English chemist Charles Hatchett and
originally named 'columbium', Niobium is a soft, grey highly
malleable metal, primarily found in the mineral pyrochlore.
Despite its relative abundance in the Earth's crust (20 parts per
million, making it the 33rd most common element), it is correctly
classified as a rare earth element, and is never found in nature in
its free metallic form.

The elements properties were so similar to those of the element
tantalum that arguments over its existence would continue for 45
years until German chemist Heinrich Rose determined that tantalum
ores did indeed contain a second element in 1846, which he called
'niobium'.

Official adoption of the elements name would come in 1949.

Niobium is used in a number of high tech industries including
microelectronics and hardened steel for pipelines. Alloys of the
metal enjoy a very high level of heat-stability, making them
particularly suitable for use in jet engines and rocket nozzles.
Additionally, a number of materials containing niobium are
super-conducting, and are used in medical imaging applications as
well as in military robotics.

Finally niobium and a number of its alloys are physiologically
inert, and as a result are used in increasing numbers of medical
devices, such as pacemakers and cybernetic implants.

Until the discovery of large quantities of niobium in Kenya in
2039, Brazil produced over 85% of the World's annual supply of
niobium.
