Soviet Union

The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR - Russian CCCP) which
shortens to the Soviet Union, was a vast state that covered over
22.4 million square kilometers of the Eurasian continent.

Founded on the 30th of December 1922 following the Russian
Revolution of 1917, the Marxist-Leninist single-party governed
state lasted for 69 years before formally dissolving on the 26th of
December 1991.

In 1941 it was drawn into the Second World War on the side of the
allies, when, despite the existence of a non-aggression pact
between the two countries, German forces invaded.

The Eastern Front as it was called by Germany, or the Great
Patriotic War as it was known in the Soviet Union lasted from 22
June 1941 through to the 9th of May 1945 and remains the largest
military confrontation in history. It is estimated that including
civilian deaths, 30 million died over that 4 year period - accounting
for approximately 43% of all the casualties in the entirety of the
Second World War.

The strain of the Eastern Front campaign would ultimately be one
of the principle reasons for the German loss of the Second World
War, and would lead to the partitioning of the country in 1945
between the Allies in the west and the Russians in the east.

Following the end of the war, disagreements emerged between the
allies as to how the new borders within Europe should be drawn
up, with the USSR tightening its grip on the Eastern Bloc; the
countries to its west and south west, while the USA would stress
that it intended to maintain a military presence in Europe and
west Germany indefinitely.

This would sow the seeds for an ideological conflict that would
grip the globe for nearly the next 50 years, as each side pursued
strategies intended to contain or weaken each other's influence in
different parts of the world.

Backed by the threat of mutually assured destruction from their
rapidly growing nuclear stockpiles, the Cold War would shape the
second half of the 20th Century.

After a period of internal liberalization in the 1980s, a series of
revolutions that over-threw numerous Communist regimes in
Eastern Europe and the reunification of East and West Germany
in 1990, the USSR would finally dissolve in 1991 - it's constituent
nations reverting to self-rule.

Following the collapse of the USSR, Russia would inherit the USSR's
seat on the United Nations.
