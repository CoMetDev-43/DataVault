ICBM

An Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (ICBM) is designated as a
missile with a minimum range of more than 5,500 kilometers.
ICBMs are classified as having greater speed and range than other
ballistic missile types: intermediate-range ballistic missiles
(IRBMs), medium-range ballistic missiles (MRBMs) and
short-range ballistic missiles (SRBMs).

These shorter range missiles are known as theatre ballistic
missiles.

World War II

The first designs for an ICBM were the German A9/A10, intended
to reach New York from a Launchpad inside German controlled
territories. The A9/A10 were developed as part of Projekt Amerika,
by Wernher von Braun under who created the V-2 rocket that saw
wide use in World War II to bomb British and Belgian cities. After
the war, von Braun relocated to the United States through
Operation Paperclip and continued his work developing IRBMs,
and ICBMs for the US Army.

Cold War

After the war ended both the United States and Russia began
research programs based on the German designs. The United
States began researching ICBMs in 1946 with the MX-774 project.
The project began underfunded, the U.S. Air Force was focused on
the use of intercontinental bombers at this time; it wasn't until the
Russians began testing their first hydrogen bomb in 1953 that the
Air Force felt a great need for an ICBM .

The Russian R-7s first launch took place on 15 May 1957. The first
successful flight of an Atlas missile occurred 28 November 1958.
The armed version of the Atlas was declared operational in
January 1959 at Vandenberg Air Force Base. The first test flight
was carried out on 9 July 1959, and the missile was put into service
on 1 September that same year.

The Russian missile program suffered a large setback 17
November 1963 at Baikonur Cosmodrome when a rocket carrying
the Soyuz 2 exploded just after liftoff destroying the launch pad
and killing many of the scientists at the facility.

The strategic theory of Mutually Assured Destruction pushed the
deployment of ICBMs throughout the Cold War. In 1972 the SALT
treaty froze the number of ICBM launchers operated by the USSR
and the United States.

Post-Cold War

After the START I treaty of 1991 both the Soviet Union and the
United States began reducing their deployed ICBMs and warhead
stockpiles. The 1990's saw the testing of functional ballistic missile
defense systems (BMD). It wasn't until 2023 that the first large
scale BMD was deployed by the United States on Aegis Cruisers,
and was followed by the Russian system going online the same
year. These systems began to be replaced by Megawatt class Directed
Energy Air Defenses (DEAD) in 2035.

On 7 July 2029 Russia successfully knocked out an operating
Communications Satellite using a repurposed ICBM. 3 days later,
the USA used an Aegis Cruiser to destroy a commercial Russian
rocket in its boost phase carrying 3 GLONASS Satellites.
