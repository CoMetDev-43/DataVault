Axis Powers

The Axis powers, also known simply as the Axis, were the
opposition forces against the United States, Great Britain and their
allies during World War II.

Formed as a military alliance in 1940 under the Tripartite Pact, the
core membership of the Axis consisted of Germany, Japan and
Italy with a fluid membership of 15-17 affiliate and client states
throughout the duration of World War II.

The core members of the Axis, Germany, Japan and Italy were
ruled by dictators.

Motivation

Their ideological opposition to Western powers and the Soviet
Union's communist doctrine unified the nations fighting for the
Axis. Although they were united in name, the Axis powers never
developed significant foreign or military policies together. Their
main interests came in the form of territorial expansion and
military conquest.

At its peak, the Axis occupied and controlled large portions of
Southeast Asia, Europe and the majority of northern Africa. This
control often came with the use of brutal military force.

Rise to Power

During the 1930's, the core members of the Axis began to
modernize and strengthen their armies. The leaders of the Axis
executed this plan to prepare for their eventual territorial
expansion but also to help lift their own countries out of the
turbulent economic downturn that occurred during the Great
Depression.

In 1933, the German chancellor's meteoric rise to power came
with the promise of revenge on those countries that helped defeat
Germany in the First World War.

In Italy, Mussolini and his Fascist party gained momentum with
many supporters by promising to bring law & order to the country
and also help rebuild Italy's economy. By 1935, Mussolini initiated
his plan for a new Holy Roman Empire by taking over Ethiopia
and expanding Italy's foothold into the African continent. By 1940,
Italy joined the Axis as the German defeat of France became
apparent.

In 1937, Japan invaded China, kicking off a full scale war.
However, China was not the limit of Japan's imperial aspirations.
After pursuing territorial expansion into French Indochina, the
United States decided to impose an oil embargo on Japan in an
effort dissuade the Japanese government from their continued
aggression.

With the German invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, the
Japanese decided to strike the United States instead of
coordinating an attack on the eastern front of the Soviet Union.
This decision led to the attack on the United States Pacific Fleet in
Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941.

Following the attack on Pearl Harbor the United States declared
war on both Japan and Germany officially initiating conflicts in
two major theaters of war.

Road to Collapse

After nearly seven years of all-out war, the Axis began to lose
ground as the Allied forces closed in on major fronts in Africa and
Europe.

In 1942, the Italian army lost a series of battles against British led
allied operations in North Africa. The Allied forces were successful
at reclaiming major positions held by the Axis since early 1941.

As a result of the operations from 1942-43, Italy was the first of the
Axis powers to surrender on September 8, 1943. Their defeat came
just six weeks after Benito Mussolini was deposed by his own
Italian Fascist party.

In early 1943, the Allies led once again by the British and
American forces began a mammoth bombing offensive against
Germany which was a strategic campaign to disrupt the fledgling
German economy, destroy German morale and displace the
German civilian population.

In spring of 1944, Soviet troops marched west towards Germany.
The nations of Romania, Bulgaria, Finland and Hungary fell soon
thereafter.

On June 6, 1944 a key victory against the German army in
Normandy, France led to an Allied foothold within Western
Europe.

By December of 1944, the German army was in retreat at the
Battle of the Bulge and by spring of 1945 the Allied forces were
crossing the Rhine while the Soviet Army encircled Berlin for their
final offensive.

With the Soviet Army marching toward victory in Berlin, the
German Chancellor committed suicide in his underground
bunker beneath the city. Seven days later on May 7, 1945,
Germany officially surrendered to the allied nations.

Finally on September 2, 1945, the last member of the alliance,
Japan, who had been fighting alone for nearly five months,
formally surrendered on the deck of the USS Missouri and the Axis
was officially dissolved.
