Sub-Cairo-Aquifer Development Project

The Sub-Cairo-Aquifer Development Project was an ill-fated
engineering project that was intended to provide drought relief to
the residents of Cairo and the surrounding areas, in a bid to stave
off an impending water resource based war between Egypt and
the nations of the Nile River Coalition (NRC).

When the remainder of the NRC nations followed Kenya into the

Common Defense Pact (CDP) in 2040 chasing promises of funds
for local dam projects along their stretches of the Nile, Egypt
voiced its concern that earlier regional treaties on water rights
were being violated.

With the dam projects set to become a reality and water levels in
the Egyptian Nile already starting to fall, in 2048 a joint project
funded by Egypt's Winslow Accord allies, the USA, Saudi-Arabia
and Japan was embarked upon; an ambitious program to extend
and exploit the natural subterranean sand-stone aquifers under
the city of Cairo and the surrounding desert.

Gargantuan mobile water refineries were constructed. Huge, slow
moving, tracked vehicles; part drilling and mining platform, part
silt separation and water purification system, intended to drill
down into the porous sand-stone vault systems in the underlying
bedrock, both expanding and extending them, in order to provide
ample drinking water for the Egyptian population.

With global temperatures continuing to rise as a result of climate
change, the Sahara desert encroached ever deeper into populated
regions of Egypt, leading to the migration and concentration of
the Egyptian population along the banks of the Nile and into Cairo
itself; numerous villages and towns were evacuated and given up
to the shifting sands.

In 2050 mega-scale engineering projects began in Cairo to house
the relocated population; massive, self-contained tower blocks
reaching up to 200 stories into the sky.

As the NRC nation's CDP funded Nile dam projects began to come
online, starting with Kenya's in 2053 and Sudan's in 2055, the
amount of water reaching Egypt fell by 20% and tensions rose as
Egyptian agriculture began to fail.

Despite the promise of the Aquifer project, when Ethiopia
completed its dam in 2059, Egypt now barely able to meet its
water requirements would declare war on the NRC ; the first war in
modern history to be fought over water rights.

Two years into the conflict, the Sub-Cairo-Aquifer project began
delivering water to the thirsty population, but the relief provided
to the nation-at-war was to be short lived. After only 1 year of
operation, the structurally weakened aquifers began to collapse
causing earth-tremors and subsidence in Cairo and its surrounds
that put the new water system out of commission and caused
major structural damage to the city's towering housing projects.

With its parched populace rioting over water shortages, its
home-front broken, the Egyptian army would retreat from NRC
territory in 2063, now fighting an emboldened invading military
force. Cairo is presently under siege by NRC forces and is not
expected to hold for much longer.

The mobile water refineries have been abandoned, left to be
reclaimed by the sands of the Sahara.
