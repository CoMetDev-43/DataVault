MKOFTEN

Project MKOFTEN was the codename for a clandestine program
developed by the CIA and United States Department of Defense
during the 1960's. The goal of MKOFTEN was to explore the
behavioral and toxicological effects of various drugs on animals
and humans.

It is generally considered one of the successors to MKULTRA, the
CIA's covert mind control program.

History

Initiated by the CIA's technical services branch, the program's
initial conceit was to develop a series of chemical compounds that
could be used on opposition targets. The agency had two specific
weapons in mind. The first was a compound that could simulate a
heart attack or stroke, the second a hallucinogen that would create
uncontrollable activity. Along with these two weapons, the CIA
also hoped to develop an immunization to help counter the effects
on their own operatives.

MKOFTEN has been noted as ground zero for the development of

Agent 15 or Zombie Gas that was used during the first Gulf War in
1990. This compound, sometimes known as EA 3167, was an
incapacitating agent that induced long-lasting delirium and
blocked nerve pathways, impairing muscle control resulting in a
target's "zombie" like state.

According to documents released around 2007, the program was
not only experimenting with chemicals and drugs but also
exploring the inner workings of the human mind. CIA officials
were rumored to have recruited psychics, clairvoyants,
astrologists, mediums, as well as specialists in the occult in an
effort to explore the world of black magic and harness the forces of
darkness.
