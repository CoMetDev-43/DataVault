FIRST DNI GAME UNVEILED

Author: Rich Tracy

Date: July 2060

I thought I had experienced virtual reality before I tried DNI gaming. To give you some
background, I received a consumer DNI implant as part of Coalescence's Voyager
Program, and have been using it in daily life for the past six months. I have chronicled this
in my tech blog " Techie in the Shell "; even in this early beta stage the DNI has changed my
life in significant and fundamental ways.

Last year Cortical Systems announced their modified gaming system that interfaces with
DNI. Being one of the few tech journalists that have a DNI, they invited me to their studio
to take a "look" at it in action.

I didn't know what to expect when walking into Cortical Systems. What does the next
generation of virtual reality hardware look like? I was expecting something out of Gabriel
Warner's Neuro_Hacker series; maybe a large tube of strange looking liquid attached to
huge banks of computers with an ominous looking helmet, but what I was presented with
was a normal office space and a standard office chair. The tech standing in the room
informed me the connection to the computer running the simulation would be made
wirelessly to my DNI.

After I authenticated the connection, I was free to explore this new virtual world.

Three demos were available, Fort Assault, City Park, and Zombie Attack. The Cortical
Systems tech recommended City Park for my first immersion. After selecting it the room
around me fell away and suddenly I was sitting in a bench near a small lake. I could feel
the breeze and smell the water. The sensation was otherworldly; it felt like I was standing
in a moving painting. After the slight disorientation went away, I stood up and walked to
the bank of the lake. I knew that my physical body was sitting in an office chair at Cortical
Systems in Virginia, but it felt in every way like I was walking along a lakeside park.

Touch, Taste, Smell, Sight and Sound, they call this immersion, I call it magic. Previous VR
headsets I have used were just shades of virtual reality, simulacra that asked you to do
all of the fooling of your brain into believing in the magic, now that magic is real. During
the demo, I found the need to take a moment to remind myself that this wasn't reality,
because all my senses were telling me otherwise. Once I had spent some time walking
around the lake I ended the simulation. The best description I can give is that it was like
waking from a dream. Opening my eyes and seeing the office where I started was both
startling and re-assuring at the same time, the virtual world I left was almost
indistinguishable from the reality now in front of me.

"A little disorientation is normal, it will pass. It's the closest thing to an out of body
experience there is." The Cortical Systems tech said as he manned the computer in the
room. He went on to explain the technology behind their virtual reality, "The key to making
these simulations so real is with the DNI we can have the user's brain do the heavy
lifting. We do not create the lake and park we just instruct your mind to create the
images. Not only does this vastly simplify the creation, it will always seem the most
believable to the end user."

Eager to experience some gameplay, I re-connect and load the Castle Assault demo.
Appearing before me was a sloped battlefield filled with archers and knights on
horseback, in the distance I could see a castle surrounded by a moat, the world sits
frozen in time, a commander approached, walking through the battlefield like a ghost,
and proceeded to brief me on the assault of the castle and asked me for the plan of
attack.

Laying out the assault on a map, I told the commander where to place the archers,
knights and siege weaponry. I was then struck by the furious sounds of battle as
everything suddenly came to life. Running onto the battlefield I led a group of knights into
a conflict with enemy pikemen. I could feel the weight of the sword, the impacts on my
armor; I even felt out of breath. It was all so real, only when I was hit with a crossbow and
fell to the ground and left my body did the shock of battle wear off.

Returning to the office and my body, the significance of this leap in virtual worlds finally
hit me. With the use of DNI we can now create realties that are indistinguishable from the
real world. The potential for the uses of this technology go far beyond recreation. Cortical
Systems told me they are working on a war games simulation for members of the
Winslow Accord, and I can only imagine how useful something like that could be. As I
stood up from the chair the tech asked I wanted to do another immersion, I told him I
would have to try their Zombie mode later, and the need to write down my experience took
priority. I remember seeing DNI for the first time, and wondering if I had witnessed a
world changing innovation, now I have one, and can say with certainly the world will never
be the same.
