Haptics

Haptics is the technological field that focuses on recreating or
simulating the sensation of touch by applying either forces,
vibrations or imparting motion to the user.

It is intended to heighten the realism of, or increase the reality of
interactions with virtual objects or interfaces in a computer
generated simulation.

The word haptic comes from the Greek work haptikos meaning
'pertaining to the sense of touch'.

Early applications of Haptic technology included force feedback
applied to the joysticks of planes using fly-by-wire systems, where
there was no longer a physical coupling between the controls and
the control surfaces that they operated - giving the pilots back a
sense of the forces operating on their plane and letting them know
when certain maneuvers were causing potentially dangerous
stresses to the vehicle.

Haptics is also employed in video game controllers, allowing a
player to feel the recoil of a fired gun, or the rumble of a road
surface.

Experimentally, Haptics have also been employed to help blind
people sense the environment around them. A system was
developed in the early 21st century consisting of a tight fitting
vest, on the back of which were a large number of inflatable
bladders in a grid. These 'cells' formed pixels - which when hooked
up to a camera, would form a 'sensory picture' on the wearers
back. With training, blind users of the system could 'see' well
enough to navigate a complex environment.

Cybernetic implants have since bypassed this particular
application of the technology.
