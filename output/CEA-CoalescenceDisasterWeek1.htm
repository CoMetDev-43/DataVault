COALESCENCE DISASTER - WEEK 1

06/09/60 - 15:00 - SINGAPORE

Unprecedented Catastrophe

It is now one week since the events that unfolded in Singapore the world,
and the scale of the disaster is still becoming clear even as the causes of
the incident remain unknown.

Early unofficial figures released by the Singapore authorities suggest that up
to 65,000 civilians lost their lives on the first day, either by proximity to the
explosion itself or due to the effects of the unknown toxins and
contaminants that were released into the atmosphere.

That number remains hard to estimate, as the decontamination experts
manning the makeshift hospitals, mortuaries and morgues that have sprung
up all over the newly established Quarantine Zone report that people are still
dying in large numbers, and they are no closer to being able to isolate the
responsible agent; in all likelihood no single toxin is to blame, but rather a
cocktail of contaminants both from the active laboratories of the Defense
Company, the Coalescence Corporation, as well as the byproducts of the
fires that at this time are still burning out of control in the areas adjacent to
the Marina Science City.

Due to the uncertain nature of the threat the quarantine conditions in place
are likely to remain for some time, in order to contain any potentially
contagious biological agent.

One thing is certain; this disaster is an unprecedented catastrophe in terms
of the number of civilian dead.

Cut off from the outside world

The established quarantine zone now encompasses an area of around 25
square miles. Armed men in hazmat gear are preventing anyone other than
medical personnel from passing in and out. There are daily reports of
shootings.

To all intents and purpose, the estimated 300,000 remaining residents of the

quarantine zone are cut off from the outside world.

Climate Scientists studying weather models of the prevailing winds on the
day of the disaster are quoted as saying "Despite how appalling the
situation on the ground is, we may have dodged a bullet. According to our
models, the easterly winds blowing at the time will have blown the toxic
cloud away from the mainland. There's no telling how many lives that may
have saved."
