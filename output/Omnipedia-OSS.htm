OSS

The OSS or Office of Strategic Services was a United States
intelligence agency created to centralize and streamline espionage
efforts by multiple branches of the US Armed services in World
War II.

Formed on June 13, 1942 by a Presidential military order, the OSS's
main functions were to coordinate wartime espionage activities
between the US armed forces, collect intelligence, engage in
wartime propaganda, as well as perform sabotage and spying
missions behind enemy lines.

The OSS played major wartime roles in operations and activities
around the globe. Detachments served from Burma to Europe,
performing a myriad of activities including arming, training and
supplying resistance movements against the Axis powers.

Some of the greatest accomplishments of the OSS were its
infiltration into Germany during World War II. This included
recruiting German and Austrian operatives for a range of special
missions, including sabotage and infiltration into German
diplomatic circles.

The OSS was officially listed has having a total of 13,000 members,
but at its height employed nearly 24,000 people worldwide.

Dissolved on September 20, 1945, the OSS ultimately became the
foundation of what is now the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA).
