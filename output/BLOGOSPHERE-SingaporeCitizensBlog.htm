It's been nearly four years since we flew out on that big green plane. I don't
know how we were lucky enough to make it...but somehow my little brother
Tsui and I got out just before the airlift ended.

Hundreds of adults were screaming at us, hateful things, really mean things,
things I would never say to anyone...not even on my worst day. I remember
pulling my legs to my chest, making myself as small as I could on the flat
back seat of that big plane because I was afraid those people might claw
their way through that cargo door and throw me off.

I don't blame them for the things they said though. They were scared just like
me and Tsui.

Not every adult was yelling at us. A few moms and dads had given up their
seats. They were just pushing through the others to get their kids to the back
door.

One lady... all I remember was her face... had these really intense eyes...
tears rolling down her cheeks. She had a really stern look on her face, like
one of the soldiers onboard, when she lifted her daughter up to the soldier at
the back door. She was so determined and just pushed her into his arms. I
didn't understand why she was pushing so much, like she couldn't wait to get
rid of her daughter. I didn't realize what was happening back then. That she
was just trying to get her on that plane and get her out that awful mess.

The mom, her name was Mae. I remember her because she gave me a
small scarf with pink flowers and kittens on it to wipe my brother's mouth off
when we rode from the camp to the airport. He always seemed to have
chocolate all over his face during that age, I guess because the soldiers
gave us so much candy.

Eunice (who I think was 4 at the time) was Mae's daughter.

Eunice was wrapped so tightly around Mae's arm it was leaving scratch
marks on her skin. I think the soldier saw Mae's face and felt sorry for her. He
scooped Eunice up and sat her next to me on the plane. Eunice was so tiny
then. It looked like she hadn't eaten in weeks.

When Mae saw me on the plane she waved to me and motioned to pick up
Eunice. I did. She cried at first because I don't think she recognized me.

When the doors started to close I could see Mae's eyes start to cry more.
Her eyes were staring at Eunice the whole time...even as the people outside
started pushing and shoving her towards the back door. The crowds of
people seemed to swirl together like one big angry wave. People were
crying, screaming, pushing and some even fighting to get closer to us.

The soldiers had to hit some of them, I think because they were trying to pull
the door open. I was too scared to keep looking at Mae, so I closed my eyes
and rubbed Popo (my stuffed penguin) as the door shut.

Eunice cried so loud, I thought I would go deaf. I just pulled her tight and
held her to my chest. My brother was next to me, sucking his thumb like he
always did when he was scared.

The engine on that plane was so loud. It rumbled forever or at least it
seemed like that to me and Eunice. I ended up holding on to her as tightly as
she did to me. My brother seemed oblivious to everything after one of the
soldiers gave him his second candy bar.

After what seemed to be minutes, we rumbled down the runway and jumped
into the air. The soldiers were standing up and held on to these leather
straps, I think they were even a little nervous because the plane jumped so
violently into the air.

The plane felt so big but it was jammed with so many of us. I remember
seeing other girls from my school. They were huddled on the floor, crying
together or praying.

A few adults made it from my neighborhood. Grandma Min, who felt ancient
but was maybe 75, was holding her twin grand babies (Lai and Alvin). They
seemed to cry non-stop as soon as we took off.

Grandma Min was a nice old lady, always very calm and would offer up a
hug at a moment's notice. She would check on Tsui, Eunice and I as well as
the other children during the flight. Shuffling along in her old muddy purple
slippers, I guess she was in a hurry and didn't have time to change her
shoes.

But then again, we were all in a hurry when that green cloud started to roll
through the city.

The horrible image of Kalin still haunts me to this day. I don't know why he
just walked into the cloud. I guess because he was curious. What it did to
him... I... I'm not sure I'm even ready to commit to paper.
