WALL HUNG CARPET

This wall hanging Mamluk Rug was found on an apartment wall
in Lotus Towers. It looks old; it’s possible that the family that
owned it brought it with them from an outlying town or village
when they were relocated to Cairo as the Sahara reclaimed their
old home. It could be around 200 years old.

The rug is exquisitely made, although much like Lotus Towers has
seen better days and is now battered and torn. Its pile was
laboriously knotted by hand, each square inch comprising around
100 tied knots. The fading colors will have been created using dyes
extracted from natural resources, the reds from the shells of
cochineal insects and the yellows from saffron or turmeric.

The Mamluks first arrived in Egypt in the 13th Century, and by
the 15th Century established a thriving carpet industry in Cairo.

Hand crafted rugs are common throughout the entire Middle East,
and are thought to have originated in Nomadic cultures that
wandered the deserts, acting as status symbols as well as serving
the practical task of providing ground cover inside their tents
when they pitched camp.
