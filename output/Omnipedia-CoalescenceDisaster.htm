Coalescence Corporation Disaster

On the morning of the 2nd of June 2060 at a little after 08:15 an
explosion occurred at the Coalescence Corporation's R&D facility,
based in part of the Underground Science City development on
the North bank of Singapore's marina.

The explosion sparked multiple fires, which sent thick plumes of
smoke rising high into the sky over the town.

Initial reports were confused, but it became clear that there was
some unknown toxin being released that was causing mass
casualties.

As the scale of the disaster became apparent the authorities
reacting rapidly, instituting emergency protocols designed to be
deployed in the event of a biological terrorist attack; a quarantine
zone was established that encapsulated roughly 25 square miles of
the cities costal region.

Reacting to the rising panic, and fearing a potentially contagious
biological agent at work, the Singapore Armed Forces enforcing
the quarantine fired on civilians attempting to flee the zone.

Specialist hazmat suited emergency response teams were
dispatched into the area, and after the first day the scale of the
catastrophe was becoming clear; there were up to 400,000 people
trapped inside the quarantine zone, and while no official numbers
had been released, early indications were that tens of thousands
had perished in the first few hours following the explosion;
already a statistic that would place this event at the head of the list
of losses of human life during peacetime.

By the end of the first week official figures were published
showing that over 100,000 were known dead, at least two thirds of
which were likely to have perished in the first hours following the
explosion.

With autopsies being carried out around the clock in make-shift
morgues all over the Quarantine Zone, no single toxin or
contaminant had been found or identified that would account for
either the number of deaths, or the symptoms reported - those
killed in the first few days appeared to have been exposed to a
powerful caustic agent; cases of burns and blistering were so
severe that dental and DNA matches were required for
identification.

It was obvious however from the corpses of those who had
survived exposed for numerous days that other toxins - either
from chemicals stored in the labs, released from the buildings as
they burned, or even from novel chemical reactions going on in
the air as various compounds combined, were also taking their toll.

On the first month anniversary of the disaster the official death
toll stood at 200,000, and people in the Quarantine Zone were still
dying. The Coalescence Corporation was co-operating fully with
the authorities, and was paying for much of the relief effort, while
helping air drop supplies for the survivors still contained within
the affected area.

By the end of August 2060, the death rate amongst the
approximately 200,000 remaining residents of the Quarantine
Zone had fallen dramatically, with the vast majority of deaths now
being attributed to aggressive, run-away cancers and damage
caused by lengthy exposure to cytotoxic contaminants.

As an infectious agent had not been ruled out, the Singapore
Authorities announced that the quarantine would stay in place for
the foreseeable future.

Building work was under way to replace the temporary fence and
guard post structures erected in the first few weeks with the
imposing walls and check points that we find on the borders of the

Quarantine Zone today.

The members of the board of the Coalescence Corporation were
by now embroiled in closed sessions with the Singapore
Authorities and the United Nations intended to determine both
cause and culpability for the accident.

The proceedings of those meetings, which symbolically closed on
the one year anniversary of the accident, remain sealed, with the
Coalescence Corporation citing business confidentiality issues for
them remaining so.

Speculation on the internet relating to the impartiality of the
proceedings remains rife.

To date no direct single cause for the deaths, especially those in
the first few hours has been publically identified, and no
prosecutions have been brought for an incident which at the
one-year-mark has cost the lives of over 300,000 civilians.

The Coalescence Corporation has spent billions of dollars on aid
for the survivors within the Quarantine Zone as well as in
payments to family members of those killed who live outside of it.

They continue to trade as one of the top performing companies on
global stock markets.
