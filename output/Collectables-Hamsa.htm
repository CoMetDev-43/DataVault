HAMSA

The Hamsa is a wall decoration in the shape of a hand, and is
commonly found throughout the Middle East and most of North
Africa. The name translates as "5" and refers to the number of
fingers on the hand.

They are symbols of protection, and are hung to either promote
good luck and prosperity, or to ward off 'the evil eye'.

The symbol is ancient; the earliest examples were found in
archaeological digs at Mesopotamian sites.

This one was found in an abandoned apartment in Lotus Towers
in Cairo.

Analysis: Kane, R

That particular Hamsa is at least 200 years old. It's quite likely that
it was passed down through the generations by the family who
lived in the apartment where you found it.

The mega-structures in Cairo were originally constructed to offer
luxury living, but as the Sahara encroached and the Nile dried up
due to the NRC 's ongoing dam projects, greater and greater
numbers of Egypt's population converged on Cairo – either
voluntarily or as part of enforced relocation programs. The towers
became vertical refugee camps.

Many of them were damaged or destroyed when the sub-Cairo
aquifer project collapsed.

The last family occupying that apartment likely brought this
Hamsa from their original village or town, as a memory of home.
