MG34

The MG-34 (Maschinengewehr 34) was a German machine gun
first developed in 1929, which was deployed in huge numbers
during the Second World War.

In its day it was considered to be the most advanced machine gun
in the world. It was light enough to be carried by a single soldier,
and could fire up to 900 rounds of 7.92 x 57mm ammunition per
minute.

With a muzzle velocity of 762 m/s and effective firing range of over
1200 meters, the weapon was deployed in a large number of both
offensive and defensive applications; offensively with a 75 round
capacity drum magazine ported by a crew of one, defensively it
was often seen tripod mounted and belt fed.

A variant (the MG-34 Panzerlauf) was used as a secondary
armament on the majority of German tanks during World War 2,
including the Tiger II.
