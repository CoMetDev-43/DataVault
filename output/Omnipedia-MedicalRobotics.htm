Medical Robotics

Advances in medical robotics have allowed surgeons to perform
more complex surgeries faster, more effectively, with higher
success rates and shorter recovery times than ever before.

Surgical robotic systems allow surgeons to access internal organs
and structures through small 'key hole' incisions, resulting in
lower infection rates and less damage to surrounding tissues.

Additionally, surgical robots have very steady hands and are
capable of incredibly accurate movements; coupled with internal
optical feeds and external real-time medical imaging they allow
the operator to perform micro-surgical procedures with far higher
precision than they could accomplish 'by hand'.

Robotic systems also allow specialists to operate by telepresence,
conducting surgeries from remote locations, reducing the
requirement for specialist surgical centers, and increasing the
likelihood that a patient has local access to the care that they
require.

As Artificial Intelligence has improved, so too have the control
systems embedded in Surgical Robots; many routine operations
can now be carried out automatically, or rather, now a human
surgeon is capable of directing the machines at a much higher
level, commanding them to perform entire steps of a procedure,
rather than having to control each individual movement
themselves.
